Mouthtype and Apple Dictation.

Start with what your Mac already does.
Add another tool when its workflow earns the space.

Apple gives you a useful starting point.

Built-in Dictation enters text in editable fields, supports customizable shortcuts and offers automatic punctuation in supported languages. General text dictation can use on-device processing on supported setups; check the notice under Dictation in Keyboard settings. Apple’s macOS 26 Dictation guide ↗

Stay with Apple if…

You want built-in dictation without another app, and its output and controls already suit your day. It’s a sensible first thing to try.

Try Mouthtype if…

You want a dedicated hold-and-release workflow, explicit cleanup choices and a small recording pill you can make your own. You’re comfortable testing an early Mac app.

Know what you’re trying.

Mouthtype 0.4.0 is a stable release. Recognition, names and insertion into individual apps can need correction. This isn’t a claim that it’s more accurate or faster than Apple Dictation. The Mac app includes per-app tone and a Settings window. Wider compatibility and fresh-install testing are still in progress.

Apple details checked against its macOS 26 guide on October 16, 2026. Language and device support vary.
